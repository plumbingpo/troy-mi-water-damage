Bathroom Water Damage Help in Troy

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. We help Troy homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Rot Under the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.

A Failed Shower Pan

Every use of the shower sends water beneath its floor when the pan or liner is cracked. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Slow Vanity Drips

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.
