Restoration Challenges Grand Rapids Homeowners Face

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. Our crews in Grand Rapids follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. If the toilet bowl was the source, assume the water is unsanitary and have the area disinfected.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. On these calls in Grand Rapids, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. If the tank lets go, shut the cold inlet valve over the heater and turn off its power or gas supply. Our Troy Restoration crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear. People mean well in the first hour after a leak, and some of what they do makes things worse. Here are the slip-ups we run into most on Grand Rapids jobs, with the better option for each.
