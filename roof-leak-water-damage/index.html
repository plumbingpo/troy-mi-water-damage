Roof Leak Water Damage in Troy: What to Know

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Catch the DripsPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingA ceiling that sags with trapped water can be drained: stay off to the side and make a small opening at its lowest spot, with a bucket underneath.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.
Coverage

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Fasteners working loose over time, so water follows the nail shank into the attic.
  • Rubber boots around plumbing vent pipes that split after years in the sun.
  • Flashing that has rusted, lifted or pulled away around chimneys, vents and walls.
  • Roof valleys where worn metal or piled debris lets water get under the covering.
  • Skylight seals and curbs that dry out, crack and let rain run inside.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Cavities

Water that reaches the top of a wall runs down inside it, wetting insulation and the back of the drywall. Bubbling paint or a swollen baseboard may be the only sign.

Roof Framing

Rafters, trusses and decking that get wet again and again begin to darken, swell and soften. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.

Mold in the Attic

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. It may take hold in 24 to 48 hours and work its way over the underside of the sheathing.

Lights and Wiring

Because fixtures are holes in the drywall, a leak often shows up at a light or fan first. Moisture in a fixture can cause a short and later corrosion, which is why it needs inspection before use.
