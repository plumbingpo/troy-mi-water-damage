Attic Water Damage: What Troy Homeowners Should Know

Attic water damage comes from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. In Troy, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Black or white fuzzy growth on the sheathing, often heaviest near the eaves
  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • A patch of insulation that is flatter, more matted, or darker than the rest
  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
Our Process

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. How far it spread is shown by moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Framing

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Fix the Cause

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

Mold Under the Roof Deck

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

Wet Wiring

Attics hold junction boxes, recessed light housings and cable runs. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

Lost Insulation Value

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. Energy bills climb, and the rooms below the wet area are hotter or colder than the rest.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

A few weeks on

Ceiling Stains Appear

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

Months later

Rot Sets In

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.
