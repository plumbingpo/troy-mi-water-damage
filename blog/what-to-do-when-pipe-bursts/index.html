Steps to Take When a Pipe Bursts in Troy, MI

Home / Blog / Burst Pipe Emergency Guide in Troy, MI

For most homeowners, a pipe bursting ranks among the worst plumbing emergencies there are. Water pours out of a burst line at a rate of 4 to 8 gallons a minute, so within hours rooms may be flooded, possessions damaged, and building materials weakened. Act correctly in those opening minutes and you may spare yourself thousands of dollars in losses, turning a potential major restoration job into a repair you can manage. Below, Troy Restoration lays out a step by step plan that homeowners in Troy, MI can follow when it happens.

Step 1: Turn the Water Off Right Away

Your first job after a pipe breaks is simple: get the water shut off as soon as you possibly can. Damage and repair bills grow with each minute the leak runs. Step one is tracking down the valve that controls your main water line. Look by the water meter first, since that is where Troy houses tend to have it, often in a utility room, garage, or basement, or on a street-side exterior wall. To stop water throughout the house, twist the handle clockwise (righty-tighty) until it stops. When the broken pipe is easy to spot and has a dedicated valve close by, shut that one too.

Your water company can cut the supply at the meter, so call them at once if the valve is missing or will not budge. Knowing where that valve lives ahead of time, not during a crisis, matters for exactly this reason. Take a few minutes today to locate yours and make sure it operates smoothly. Should the handle be seized, get a plumber out to fix it well ahead of any emergency.

Step 3: Move and Shield What You Can

There is useful work to do until help shows up: first, carry anything valuable, plus paperwork and electronics, to a dry room. Furniture resting on damp carpet should be raised, with foil or plastic blocks set under each leg. Pick up any area rugs lying on wet flooring. Where puddles are easy to get to, blot them up with towels. Open closet and cabinet doors to promote air circulation. Turn off electricity to affected areas at the breaker panel if you can do so safely without standing in water.

Do not step into pooled water anywhere close to outlets, electrical panels, or appliances. If the burst pipe has caused significant flooding and you cannot safely access your electrical panel, leave the electricity on and wait for professionals to assess the situation. No belonging is worth risking an injury.

Step 4: Document Everything for Insurance

Hold off on cleanup until you have a thorough record of the damage to give your insurer. Take photos and video of all standing water, damaged materials, and affected belongings. If the spot where the pipe failed is visible, record it, along with the day and approximate hour you first noticed the problem. This documentation, combined with the detailed reports Troy Restoration provides, will support your insurance claim and help you seek fair compensation for the damage.

How Troy Homeowners Can Avoid Burst Pipes

Some bursts cannot be avoided, but Troy homeowners lower the odds a great deal when they insulate exposed pipes in unheated spots such as attics, crawl spaces, and garages. When temperatures drop, let faucets drip a little so water keeps moving in the lines. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to allow warm air to reach pipes. Pressure over 80 PSI calls for a pressure reducing valve, so check your reading from time to time. Braided stainless steel should take the place of worn supply lines, rubber washing machine hoses above all. And know your shutoff valve location before you need it.
