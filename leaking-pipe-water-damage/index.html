Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Troy: What to Know

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. For Troy homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. Getting it out is the work we do. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the Water SupplyClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Check the Water MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Shifting soil or abrasion wearing through water lines that run beneath a concrete slab.
  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

Leaks over a ceiling or behind a wall surface show as bubbling paint and brown stains. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

Rotted Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Flooring Damage

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. Laminate seams swell, hardwood boards cup, and the glue holding tile or vinyl releases.

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. During a slow leak, the back side of the drywall can be covered well before the painted face shows one spot.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Check your policy and ask your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Access to the pipe, with a sink cabinet being cheaper to work in than a slab or a closed wall.
  • What the floors and finishes are made of, because certain materials cannot be dried in place.
  • Whether the leak lasted days or months, and the size of the area it wet.
  • Mold, if there is any, because containment and removal come first and drying finishes after.
