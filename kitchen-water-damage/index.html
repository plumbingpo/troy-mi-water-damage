Kitchen Water Damage in Troy

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. Our Troy crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Warning Signs

  • The toe kick boards have puffed up, turned dark, or come loose
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Vinyl floor that has discolored or gone soft by the cabinets or the refrigerator
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Toe Kick Space

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Save or Replace

Dry It or Replace It?

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.
