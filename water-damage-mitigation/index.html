Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. Our Troy crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. In fact there are two separate stages. Mitigation is the emergency phase: stop the loss, limit the spread, stabilize the home. Once the structure tests dry, restoration takes over and covers repairing or rebuilding finishes, drywall and floors that were damaged. Insurance companies also bill the two as separate line items, which makes it useful to know your current phase.

A typical mitigation visit covers four things. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Extraction, so standing water is gone. Containment, using plastic barriers and controlled airflow so moisture and contaminants stay out of clean rooms. Four: stabilize by pulling materials beyond saving, applying antimicrobial to surfaces and setting up air movers plus dehumidifiers. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

How Our Water Damage Mitigation Works

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Troy Restoration:

1

Stop the Water Source

We shut off the supply, cap the failed line, or tarp and board the opening where water is entering. Should a lasting fix require a plumber or a roofer, our team coordinates when that happens.

2

Extract the Water

We extract standing water using pumps and extraction units, then check carpet and its padding. Getting bulk water out quickly does more to limit damage than any amount of drying equipment alone.

3

Contain Wet Areas

We isolate the affected rooms from the rest through airflow control and plastic barriers. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Check under sinks and around toilets monthly for drips, stains or soft flooring.
  • Before heavy rain returns, caulk openings at doors, windows and roof penetrations.
  • Get the water heater checked every year, and replace it ahead of a tank failure.
  • Have an automatic shutoff fitted, so the main closes itself once a leak is sensed.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • The leak is fixed, yet cabinets, walls or flooring remain wet.
  • Following a water loss, indoor air stays damp and condensation forms on the glass.
  • One day on, damp materials are beginning to smell musty.
  • Water went beneath kitchen or bath cabinets, out of reach of towels and fans.
  • Rain is getting inside through a damaged roof or an open window gap.
  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.
  • The carpet pad is soaked through even where the surface seems only damp.

Why Hire a Professional for Water Damage Mitigation?

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Has the water shifted category since the leak started, and is it Category 1, 2 or 3 now? Does the cabinet toe kick need to come off? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

There is a claim-related reason to do mitigation right as well. Owners are required by most policies to take reasonable steps against further damage, and the insurer will ask for evidence. We photograph conditions on arrival, record moisture readings daily and keep a list of anything removed. That file shows what the water did and what was done about it. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.
