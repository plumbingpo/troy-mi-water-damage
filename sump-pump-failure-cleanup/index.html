Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Troy

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • Stuck or snagged float switches, which never tell the pump to start.
  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • An intake screen packed with mud and stones so the pump cannot draw water.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Framing

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Carpet and Flooring

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Vinyl plank and laminate floors generally have to be pulled as well, since dirty water stays under them.

Finished Basement Walls

The wet zone inside a wall reaches well past the water mark, as drywall and insulation pull moisture up off the floor. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Stored Belongings

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Anything porous that sat in it is hard to disinfect and will grow mold within days.
