Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Troy: What to Know

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

The first thing our Troy team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. Clean water means extraction and drying. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the SupplyFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Stop FlushingNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterIf the water came from the bowl or contains waste, keep children and pets out, and wear gloves and waterproof shoes if you must walk through it.
  4. Check the Room BelowIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • Supply lines that give out or tanks that crack, releasing clean water across the floor.
  • Failed wax rings under the toilet, which leak at the floor on each flush.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
  • Blocked traps and drain lines, which leave the bowl nowhere to empty but onto the floor.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Odor That Returns

An odor can return every time the weather turns humid if contaminated water dried beneath the floor or inside the bottom of a wall, and only removing the affected material ends it.

Soaked Subfloor

Grout lines and the base of the toilet let water pass under vinyl and tile. Plywood below swells and delaminates, which can release the flooring glued to it.

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

The bottom edge of a baseboard or door casing draws in water as soon as the floor floods. Fiberboard trim, the most common kind, swells and splits and never returns to shape.

Bacterial Contamination

Carpet, pad, drywall and baseboards all pick up bacteria from bowl or drain water. Porous materials cannot be reliably disinfected and usually have to be removed.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Determine the Water Type

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Extract and Remove

Standing water is extracted first. Contaminated drywall, baseboards or carpet pad come out where necessary, and the toilet is lifted when water has reached beneath the flange and base.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

Floors, wall bases, fixtures and the subfloor are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products. When the water is Category 3, we finish this step before turning on any drying equipment.

4

Dry and Verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. We take moisture readings until the subfloor and ceiling are back to normal levels.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. If the toilet leaked at its base for months, that damage is usually not covered. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • How long the water sat before extraction and cleanup began.
  • The distance the water spread, for example to a closet, a hallway or carpet in a bedroom.
  • Bathroom flooring material, since some floors dry in place and others must be taken up.
  • Category of water, as a contaminated overflow adds labor, protective measures and disinfecting.
